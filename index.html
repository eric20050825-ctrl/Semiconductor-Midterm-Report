Strain-Engineered Bands

Stretch or squeeze a crystal, and its energy bands move.

Strain changes the distance between atoms. That small change shifts the band gap, splits energy levels that used to be equal, and moves Raman peaks. Try it yourself in the lab below.

Topic: deformation-potential theory Audience: upper-year physics undergraduates Format: interactive explainer
Part 1 · The physics in 1 minute

Why atomic spacing decides the bands

One-line summary: uniform strain shifts energy levels; directional strain splits them.

1Strain changes bond length

Stretch (tensile, ε>0): atoms move apart, orbitals overlap less.
Squeeze (compressive, ε<0): atoms move closer, orbitals overlap more.
Bands come from this overlap, so they change too.

d = d₀(1 + ε)

2Uniform strain shifts the gap

Equal strain in every direction (hydrostatic) keeps the crystal's symmetry, so levels only move up or down. Tensile → smaller gap. Compressive → larger gap.

ΔEg = a · εhydro a = deformation potential, a few eV, usually negative.

3Directional strain splits levels

Pulling along one axis breaks the symmetry. Levels that were equal now separate: Si's 6 conduction valleys → 2 + 4, and heavy / light holes split. This is how strained transistors get faster carriers.

ΔEvalley ∝ Ξu · εshear,   ΔEHH−LH ≈ 2b · εshear

4Measure it with a laser (Raman)

Atomic vibrations (phonons) feel strain too. Tensile → lower frequency, compressive → higher. Shear strain also splits the Raman peak. A Raman laser reads strain without touching the sample.

ω ≈ ω₀ + (shift) · εhydro ± (split) · εshear

On the numbers: the deformation potentials above are representative teaching values — correct in sign and order of magnitude, assembled to be consistent with the qualitative trends reported in standard references (e.g. Yu & Cardona, Fundamentals of Semiconductors; Chuang, Physics of Photonic Devices), including Ξu(Si) ≈ 9.2 eV, which is a literature-standard value. They are not a precision materials database, and the lattice sketch in Part 2 exaggerates bond-length changes (×9 by default, adjustable) so that a realistic ±2% strain stays visible. Temperature changes the unstrained gap through the Varshni relation Eg(T) = Eg(0) − αT²/(T+β) with literature α, β; the strain response itself is taken as temperature-independent. Biaxial mode assumes in-plane strain ε with the out-of-plane strain set by Poisson relaxation (ν = 0.30). The physics they encode — linear deformation-potential response, degeneracy lifted only by the shear component of strain — is the actual point of the model.
